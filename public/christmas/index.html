Sound familiar?

Most families have the same four problems.

Boxes of photos nobody sees. Albums in the closet, slides in a carousel, negatives in an envelope. Most of them haven't been looked at in years, and many can't be viewed at all without a scanner.

No one knows the stories behind them. Who is that standing next to Grandpa? Where was this house? The only person who knows is the one in the picture, and nobody has written it down.

There's never enough time. Sorting, scanning, interviewing, editing and putting it all together takes weeks. It stays on the someday list, and someday doesn't always come.

Recordings that never get heard. A long phone video with no way to find the good parts is hard to share and easy to lose.

We do all of it for you.

A conversation, your photos, and a finished family keepsake.

  1. A free 15-minute planning call. Tell us who you'd like to honor. We choose questions together, and you can add your own.
  2. We gather the photos. Send us digital photos, or we scan prints, album pages, slides and negatives (see below).
  3. We sit down and record. At home, at their pace, with professional microphones for the interviewer and the storyteller. Several short sessions are fine.
  4. We put it together. Every story becomes its own chapter, long pauses and "um"s are gently trimmed, every word is written out, and photos are placed beside the stories they belong to.
  5. Your family keeps it. A private story page to listen, read and share, plus downloads of the audio and a printable transcript. Add a printed book if you'd like one under the tree.

About the photos

However your photos are stored, we can bring them along.

Already digital. Photos on a phone, computer or in an online album. Share them with a link and we take it from there.

Loose prints. We scan them at your home during a session, or you drop them off and get every one back.

Photo albums. Old albums are often glued or fragile, so we photograph each page in place and never pull photos out.

Slides and negatives. We digitize them so you can finally see them, often for the first time in decades.

During the interview we look through the photos together, so each one comes with the story of who, where and when.

Questions people ask

How long should the interview be?

An hour covers the highlights. Three hours tells a life story, from childhood to today. Longer conversations are best split into sessions of an hour or so, so nobody gets tired.

Can it be finished by Christmas?

Yes, if the interview is recorded by mid-December. If not, give a gift certificate and we'll record in the new year.

Who can hear it?

Only the people you choose. Your story page is private, and the recording belongs to your family.

Can family members join in?

Yes. Family can send questions ahead of time, sit in on the interview, or add photos of their own.

What if they're shy or unsure what to say?

They see the questions ahead of time and can skip any of them. It feels like a relaxed conversation, not a performance.
